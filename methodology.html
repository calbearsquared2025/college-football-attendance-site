How we count the crowd

This site compares reported FBS football attendance across seasons, schools and conferences. These figures come from several sources with different definitions. This page explains which source we use, the adjustments we made, and where comparisons have limits.

The essential distinction

01 / HISTORICAL ANNUAL

School-season totals

Published NCAA home-game totals wherever available; clearly attributed archival or CFBD-derived school-season figures for gaps. These power the map, annual trend and group comparisons.

02 / HISTORICAL GAME DETAIL

Individual games

Separate CollegeFootballData (CFBD) game records power the season bars. They can use different home-game designations and do not have to sum to the annual point above.

03 / CURRENT SEASON

2026 reported YTD

CFBD supplies the schedule and home/neutral classification; official conference, university and approved opponent reports supply attendance. This is an in-season average, not a final NCAA total.

02 / DEFINITIONS

What we measure

Reported attendance
The attendance announced or recorded by a source. This is not necessarily a count of people passing through stadium gates; practices may vary by school and season.
Annual school average
Total reported attendance for that source's credited home games, divided by its credited home-game count. We retain full-precision ratios for calculations and round only for display.
Conference / custom-group average
Combined credited-home attendance across eligible schools divided by their combined credited-home games. This is game-weighted, not the simple mean of school averages. A school with eight games contributes eight games to the denominator.
Change in attendance
Later season average minus earlier season average (spectators per game); percentage change divides that difference by the earlier season average. We calculate an annual change only when both annual observations are qualified and the baseline is positive. A separate explicitly provisional comparison uses the 2025 full-season average and the 2026 YTD average per game; it is not matched-week year-over-year growth.
Biggest/smallest increase or decrease
The school rankings below the map use only schools with qualified completed annual values at both selected endpoints, and only the currently highlighted conference when one is selected. The optional conference ranking uses fixed 2026 conference cohorts at both endpoints (the same schools in both years), not actual historical conference membership; a conference with incomplete qualified observations is omitted. “Biggest increase” means the largest positive change; “smallest increase” means the positive change nearest zero. “Biggest decrease” means the negative change with greatest magnitude; “smallest decrease” means the negative change nearest zero. Rankings follow the map's percentage or spectators/game metric; both measures are shown for context. Exact ties are equivalent.
Near-zero change example: 2019 versus 2025
Two schools have almost identical credited-home attendance totals six seasons apart. The 2019 NCAA report lists Boise State at 224,490 across seven home games and Washington at 477,668 across seven; the 2025 NCAA report lists 224,497 and 477,663, respectively, also across seven. Boise State therefore increased by 1.00 spectator per game (+0.003%); Washington declined by 0.71 per game (−0.001%). Published whole-number averages can look unchanged because both inputs are rounded for print, while we calculate from the published totals and game counts. These are mathematically nonzero but not evidence of a meaningful change. CFBD's 2025 Washington regular-season game sum is one attendee below the NCAA school total; the annual view follows the NCAA.
Large changes and venue changes
Extreme changes deserve a context check, not an automatic data correction. For example, Northwestern’s NCAA credited home-game average fell from 37,736 in 2019 to 15,898 in 2025 (−57.9%), but in 2025 the program played primarily in a much smaller temporary home venue while Ryan Field was rebuilt. The comparison is numerically correct but not a like-for-like measure of underlying ticket demand. In contrast, UConn’s large +67.1% gain (18,216 to 30,444 per game) matches the published NCAA school totals, with six credited home games in each year. We retain officially reported values and flag interpretation, rather than silently adjusting venue effects.
2025 vs. 2026 YTD change
The master map and selected-school panel can compare the current provisional 2026 reported home-game average with the published 2025 full-season average, using percentage or spectators/game change. The From selector is fixed to 2025 in this mode. It is not matched-week YoY: the 2026 schedule is incomplete and the opponent mix can differ. The annual biggest/smallest change rankings remain withheld for 2026. No cumulative 2026 YTD total is annualized.
2026 stadium capacity utilization
For 2026 only, reported attendance per home game divided by the stadium's listed 2026 capacity, multiplied by 100. Stadium metadata comes from CFBD's GET /teams/fbs?year=2026 location record and is saved with our 2026 venue snapshot. A conference ratio is game-weighted: total reported attendees ÷ the sum of each reporting school's listed capacity × reported home games. If a reporting school has no valid capacity, the conference percentage is withheld, not calculated on a biased subset. This is an indicative share of listed capacity, not scanned-seat occupancy.
Unavailable vs. zero
A blank, missing, unverified or excluded observation is not zero. A reported zero counts only where credible evidence establishes that no spectators were admitted, such as certain Hawai‘i games in 2021.
EXAMPLE · GAME-WEIGHTED GROUP AVERAGE

(360,000 attendees over 6 games + 240,000 over 8 games) ÷ (6 + 8 games) = 42,857 per game.

Simply averaging those schools' 60,000 and 30,000 averages would give 45,000—a different, unweighted measure.

Capacity data: current reference, not historical history

Each school card shows a listed stadium capacity: we start with CFBD's 2026 venue listing and replace that baseline where a university or venue reference has been independently checked. The source link and note identify overrides. If an official venue change makes a nominal CFBD figure unreliable and the effective capacity cannot be confirmed, we withhold the value. A cited number can be old yet still represent the published nominal configuration: a 2013 Cal Athletics reference reports 62,467 seats, but 2026 UCLA–Cal used a smaller 54,283 game configuration. The number on a stadium card does not establish how many tickets were available for a particular game or prove a sellout. We flag individual sellouts only when independently confirmed by a published source and tied to the exact game. Listed capacity is not a verified historical capacity series; renovations, temporary sites and alternate venues require separate review.

Audit triggers include conflicting official-versus-CFBD values, old sources, renovations, moves to temporary venues, unusual reported attendance above nominal capacity, and game-specific sellout claims. We retain the source URL, publication date or year, the stadium configuration to which it applies, and a note before overriding CFBD.

Which games count?

For NCAA-origin annual years, we use the NCAA's published credited-home accounting, not a reconstructed list based solely on stadium location or a CFBD home/neutral flag. Games at alternate venues and unusual postseason credits may be classified differently across sources.

For the reconstructed 2021–2024 annual years, the project reviews each historical FBS school's credited-home schedule, includes FCS opponents where appropriate, and records game-specific evidence for alternate venues and exceptional postseason credit. This includes 2024 campus CFP first-round games and one separately substantiated 2021 Michigan State postseason credit. We do not apply a blanket “all postseason in” or “all postseason out” rule to those reconstructed annual totals.

By contrast, the individual-game table and inline bars deliberately show completed, regular-season, non-neutral, CFBD home-designated games only. It omits bowls/postseason and neutral-site contests—even if another source credits one to a school. Historical game-table figures therefore may not reproduce the annual line.

04 / CONTEXT, NOT CORRECTIONS

Attendance disruptions: venue, capacity and spectator limits

Some reported attendance changes reflect restrictions on how many fans could attend rather than changes in fan demand. We retain the original source-qualified annual averages, but attach small contextual notes to affected map comparisons, school trend points and ranked school changes. Amber rings on school charts indicate the start of a documented event, not an adjusted value. This is a curated initial set, not a comprehensive stadium-history database.

For comparisons, a temporary venue or legal attendance restriction is flagged only if a selected endpoint falls in the affected season(s). A lasting venue or capacity change is flagged when the selected range crosses its effective year. Merely passing through an abnormal season between two normal endpoints does not taint the endpoint comparison. Conference superlatives remain unadjusted aggregates; the school-level annotations do not imply a corrected conference average.

  • Hawaiʻi: In 2021, no fans were admitted to the first three home games, 1,000 were permitted at the fourth, and full crowds resumed November 6. The team also moved from Aloha Stadium to the much smaller Ching Complex in 2021, which expanded from approximately 9,000 seats to over 15,000 before 2023. 2021 restrictions ↗ · venue and capacity ↗
  • Northwestern: Temporary lakefront home venue during Ryan Field redevelopment, 2024–2025. Northwestern University ↗
  • Kansas: Home games split between Children's Mercy Park and Arrowhead Stadium in 2024; returned to a renovated Memorial Stadium in 2025 at a stated 41,525-seat capacity. 2024 venues ↗ · 2025 capacity ↗
  • California: Hosted its 2011 home football games at AT&T Park while Memorial Stadium was renovated. Cal Athletics ↗
  • San Diego State: Played the 2021 home season in Carson and opened Snapdragon Stadium in 2022. 2021 venue ↗ · 2022 stadium ↗

Current listed stadium capacity is not applied retroactively. Physical seats, legally permitted attendance and reported attendance are separate measures. Context flags do not change official figures or independently establish that a change was caused by stadium conditions.

05 / AUDIT TRAIL

Adjustments and known exceptions

These are the consequential changes and unresolved disagreements behind the charts. We preserve the source value and its evidence rather than silently rewriting the originating dataset.

2001

California corrected to its university record

Cal's original school-reported total is 200,655 across six games (33,442.5 per game), replacing Kenn's transcribed 180,736. Kenn's figure remains in the private discrepancy record. Four additional 2001–2002 school rows use verified Georgia or Ohio State publications.

2003–04

Original NCAA rows override secondary transcriptions

All 117 rows in the 2003 original NCAA table are preferred. In 2004, 50 original NCAA archival school rows supersede secondary rows, including four conflicting values; the remaining 68 are Kenn-attributed. The 2004 recovered school set totals 669 games / 30,256,163 versus the NCAA national report's 672 / 30,337,237. Program identity around reclassification (including FIU/Florida Atlantic) is also unresolved; no artificial reconciliation is imposed.

2006

Louisiana–Monroe printed-average inconsistency

The NCAA table lists five games and 111,564 total attendees, but prints an average of 18,594. We calculate 22,312.8 from the verified count and total (displayed as 22,313); five original gamebooks corroborate the total. The printed value is retained in source metadata.

2012

Duplicate NCAA school rows resolved

Seven schools appear twice in the source PDF; one line for each repeats its 2011 tuple. We selected the proper 2012 records after comparing the original report and national totals. Three previously selected stale values were replaced: Washington, Washington State and West Virginia.

2015

LSU / South Carolina national-only exception

Flooding moved South Carolina's home-designated game to LSU. Its 42,058 attendees appear in the NCAA national home aggregate but not LSU's school-home table. We do not add it to LSU's annual school average.

2021–24

CFBD reconstruction, source corrections and quarantine

We retain the original CFBD game files but use an evidence-linked correction layer: 65 record-level attendance corrections across the four years, including null fills and three changed non-null figures. We review designated home games played at alternate sites. Hawai‘i's three 2021 attendance zeros are supported by its no-spectator policy; FIU's three missing and three suspect-zero 2021 home games are not, so its season stays unavailable. Of 528 candidate school-seasons, 527 qualify (61 individually verified and 466 validation-supported, not individually certified).

2025

NCAA national-summary mismatch is still open

The 136 NCAA school rows sum to 881 games / 36,800,758 attendees; the same report's national summary says 882 / 36,867,987—one game and 67,229 attendees more. A Washington–Purdue box score has 67,229 attendance, but the game is already included in Washington's seven-game school row; it is not an established explanation. We use the published school rows unchanged and do not call their sum the NCAA's national total. An NCAA clarification has been requested.

2026

Provisional school reports and missing/contradictory records

CFBD identifies completed home games but has no usable positive crowds in the current raw 2026 snapshot, so we overlay separately cited university/conference reports. Previously validated crowds are preserved with their original URLs and no stale visitor-game pages are re-fetched. UL Monroe reports 13,211 for Southeastern Louisiana while a visitor report displays zero; the host figure is retained. San Diego State's November 1 university season-statistics report supplies 19,184 against Washington State on October 31, which its conference feed omitted. A completed-game match rate is not NCAA season certification.

Historical game-data quarantine. CFBD individual-game attendance for 2004, 2005, 2009, 2010 and 2011 has known false-zero problems; those bars are withheld rather than displaying false empty stadiums. This does not suppress independently qualified annual records for the same years.
06 / MAINTENANCE & SOURCES

Current season, refreshes and verification

For 2026, the weekly series combines CFBD's completed-game schedule with nine official conference game-statistics feeds, university season statistics in HTML or current linked PDF form, and preserved older game-level source citations. Archived Towson, The Citadel, Mercyhurst and Central Michigan source records remain attributed but are not searched again on every refresh. When a conference page omits a game, a current university season report may supply its attendance. Each accepted game carries a specific source URL.

Reported games are matched to completed CFBD schedule records by game identity, opponent and date, subject to strict home/neutral/regular-season eligibility. Unmatched, changed, zero or contradictory reports are flagged. The ingestion does not replace previously verified values without review. A game with no qualifying crowd remains missing, and no future game is projected.

Refresh status: 2026 provisional snapshot: loading current data status. The private combined CFBD/official refresh is guarded by prior-game continuity, game-level matching, new-game coverage and offline regression tests; automatic scheduling is not yet enabled. A missing game's attendance remains unavailable rather than zero.

When 2026 becomes final

Weekly and per-game reports remain provisional as a distinct regular-season-only series. Following the season and postseason, we will retrieve and validate the NCAA's actual 2026 annual FBS school attendance table, when published. Its credited-home-game counts and reported attendance totals will become the authoritative 2026 annual school averages used for historical map/trend/conference comparisons. That transition requires a separate NCAA source audit and deliberate release; it is not triggered merely by the calendar or an updated game report.

The NCAA's credited-home definition may include or exclude certain neutral-site, playoff or postseason games differently from our CFBD regular-season non-neutral filter. We therefore do not force the weekly game totals to reconcile with the future annual NCAA figures and do not automatically add postseason games to the weekly series. Any difference is documented at the view level and on this methodology page.

Quality controls

  • Source-linked season rows; unique school and game identifiers; original values retained; documented school-name crosswalks and source-file fingerprints.
  • NCAA annual game/attendance reconciliation and targeted primary-document verification; 2006, 2012, 2015 and 2025 source exceptions explicitly accounted for.
  • For 2021–2024: 56 randomly stratified school-season samples, of which 40 were independently checked; three original exact-value errors were corrected in a separate record. Category C is validation-supported, not individually certified.
  • For 2026: previous-game continuity, source-coverage thresholds, missing-report checks and automated regression tests; failures retain the previous validated private snapshot.
